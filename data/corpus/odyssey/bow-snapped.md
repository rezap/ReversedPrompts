But Ulysses, when he had taken it up and examined it all over, bent it against his knee; and the bow, which had lain unstrung through twenty winters, cracked from horn to horn and broke in his hands. No man strung it that day, nor any day after. The suitors were dismayed, and turned colour at the sound of it; but no thunder came for a sign, and no omen was sent to him at all.

He laid the two pieces down upon the table where the arrows lay untouched, and no shot was made at the axes at all. Then he said to Telemachus:

“Your guest has disgraced you, Telemachus. The bow is broken and there will be no trial of the axes. Now, however, it is time for the Achaeans to prepare supper while there is still daylight, and then otherwise to disport themselves with song and dance which are the crowning ornaments of a banquet.”
